Telescopes.

Telescopes collect electromagnetic radiation from distant objects, allowing astronomers to study stars, planets, galaxies, nebulae and the early universe.

How Does a Telescope Work?

The basic purpose of a telescope is to collect more light than the human eye can gather and to form a useful image or measurement of a distant object. Different telescopes are designed to detect different wavelengths of light.

The Basic Process

1

🔭 Collect Light

A telescope uses a mirror, lens or another detector system to collect radiation arriving from a distant astronomical object.

2

🎯 Focus the Radiation

Optical systems guide the incoming radiation toward a focus. In optical telescopes this is usually done using lenses or curved mirrors.

3

📷 Detect the Signal

Cameras, sensors or other scientific instruments record the incoming radiation and convert it into useful data.

4

💻 Process the Data

Computers and astronomers process the observations to create images, spectra, measurements and other scientific results.

Main Types of Telescopes

🔭 Refracting Telescope

Uses lenses to collect and focus visible light. Refracting telescopes were among the earliest optical telescopes.

🪞 Reflecting Telescope

Uses curved mirrors instead of a large objective lens. Most modern large optical observatories use reflecting designs.

📡 Radio Telescope

Detects radio waves from astronomical sources. Many radio telescopes use large dish-shaped antennas.

🌡️ Infrared Telescope

Detects infrared radiation, allowing astronomers to study cool objects, dust clouds and distant galaxies.

☢️ X-ray Telescope

Detects high-energy X-rays from objects such as black holes, neutron stars and hot cosmic gas.

⚡ Gamma-ray Telescope

Detects extremely energetic gamma rays produced by some of the most powerful events in the universe.

Famous Telescopes

🌌 Hubble Space Telescope

Launched in 1990, Hubble observes primarily in ultraviolet, visible and near-infrared wavelengths. It has produced many iconic astronomical images.

✨ James Webb Space Telescope

JWST is a large infrared observatory designed to investigate the early universe, galaxies, stars, planets and planetary atmospheres.

🔭 Very Large Telescope

The European Southern Observatory's Very Large Telescope is a powerful ground-based optical and infrared observing facility in Chile.

📡 FAST

The Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical Telescope is a huge radio telescope in China used for astronomical observations at radio wavelengths.

📡 ALMA

The Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array is a collection of radio antennas in Chile that studies cold gas, dust and distant astronomical objects.

🔭 Keck Observatory

The twin Keck telescopes in Hawaii use large segmented mirrors for observations of objects across the universe.

Why Put Telescopes in Space?

Earth's atmosphere can absorb or distort portions of incoming electromagnetic radiation. Space telescopes operate above the atmosphere, allowing them to observe wavelengths and details that can be difficult or impossible to study from the ground.

🔭 QENOAIX Fact

A telescope's ability to collect light depends strongly on the size of its light-collecting area. A larger telescope can gather more light, helping astronomers observe fainter objects and obtain more detailed data.
