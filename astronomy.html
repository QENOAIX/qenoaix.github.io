Planets

Explore all eight planets of our Solar System, from tiny Mercury to distant Neptune.

⭐

Stars

Stars are enormous, extremely hot spheres of plasma held together by their own gravity.

Most stars produce energy through nuclear fusion, mainly converting hydrogen into helium in their cores.

Their mass affects their temperature, brightness, lifetime and eventual fate.

🌌

Galaxies

Galaxies are enormous gravitationally bound systems containing stars, gas, dust and dark matter.

They can have different shapes, including spiral, elliptical and irregular forms.

The Milky Way is the galaxy that contains our Solar System and is a barred spiral galaxy.

🕳️

Black Holes

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing can escape once it passes the event horizon.

Some black holes form when massive stars collapse. Much larger supermassive black holes exist at the centres of many galaxies.

Scientists study black holes by observing their effects on nearby matter, light and stars.

🚀

Space Exploration

Space exploration uses spacecraft, satellites, probes, landers and rovers to investigate objects beyond Earth.

Robotic missions have explored planets, moons, asteroids and comets throughout our Solar System.

Space missions also help scientists study Earth, the Sun and the wider universe.

🔭

Telescopes

Telescopes collect electromagnetic radiation from distant objects so astronomers can study them.

Different telescopes observe different wavelengths, including visible light, radio, infrared, ultraviolet and X-rays.

Space telescopes can observe wavelengths that Earth's atmosphere absorbs or distorts.

🌠 What is Astronomy?

Astronomy is the scientific study of objects and phenomena beyond Earth's atmosphere. It helps us understand planets, moons, stars, galaxies, nebulae, black holes and the universe as a whole.

Astronomy brings together physics, mathematics, chemistry, computer science and advanced technology to investigate how the universe works.

☀️ Our Solar System

Our Solar System is made up of the Sun and everything gravitationally bound to it. It includes eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets and countless smaller objects.

The four inner planets — Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars — are rocky worlds. Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants, while Uranus and Neptune are ice giants.

⭐ Stars

Stars are enormous, extremely hot spheres of plasma. Most stars generate energy through nuclear fusion in their cores, where hydrogen is converted into helium.

A star's mass strongly influences how it evolves. Stars can have very different sizes, temperatures, colours and lifetimes.

🌌 Galaxies

A galaxy is a huge gravitationally bound system containing stars, gas, dust and dark matter.

Galaxies come in different forms, including spiral, elliptical and irregular galaxies. Our home galaxy, the Milky Way, is a barred spiral galaxy.

🕳️ Black Holes

A black hole is a region of space where gravity is extraordinarily strong. Beyond its event horizon, escaping would require a speed greater than the speed of light.

Black holes can form from the collapse of massive stars, and supermassive black holes are found at the centres of many galaxies.

💥 The Universe

The observable universe contains an enormous number of galaxies, each containing vast numbers of stars. The universe has been expanding for billions of years.

Modern cosmology uses observations, mathematics and physics to investigate the origin, structure and evolution of the universe.

🚀 Space Exploration

Space exploration uses spacecraft, satellites, landers, rovers and telescopes to study places that are difficult or impossible to reach directly.

Robotic missions have explored planets, moons, asteroids and comets, while space telescopes allow astronomers to observe distant objects across the electromagnetic spectrum.

🔭 How Do Telescopes Work?

Telescopes collect electromagnetic radiation from distant objects and produce information that astronomers can analyse.

Different telescopes observe different parts of the electromagnetic spectrum, including visible light, radio waves, infrared radiation, ultraviolet radiation and X-rays.

🧠 Did You Know?

Looking farther into space also means looking farther into the past. Because light takes time to travel, astronomers see distant objects as they were when their light began its journey toward us.

🌠 The Big Question

How did the universe begin, how will it evolve, and are we alone in it? Astronomy combines physics, mathematics and technology to investigate questions like these.
