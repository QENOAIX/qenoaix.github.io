Types of Galaxies.

Galaxies are enormous collections of stars, gas, dust, planets, and dark matter held together by gravity. They come in several major shapes and structures.

The Major Types

🌀 Spiral Galaxies

Spiral galaxies have a flat rotating disk with a central bulge and curved spiral arms. The arms often contain large amounts of gas and dust where new stars can form.

🥚 Elliptical Galaxies

Elliptical galaxies range from nearly spherical shapes to stretched ellipsoids. They generally contain older stars and much less cold gas and dust than spiral galaxies.

🌌 Irregular Galaxies

Irregular galaxies do not have a well-defined spiral or elliptical structure. Their unusual shapes can sometimes result from gravitational interactions with other galaxies.

🌀 Barred Spiral Galaxies

Barred spiral galaxies are a special type of spiral galaxy. A straight bar of stars crosses their central region, with spiral arms extending from the ends of the bar.

Galaxy Types Explained

Spiral Structure

Spiral galaxies typically have a central bulge, a disk, spiral arms, and a surrounding halo. Our own Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy.

Elliptical Structure

Elliptical galaxies have smooth, rounded appearances. Their stars generally follow more random orbital paths rather than moving mainly in a thin disk.

Irregular Structure

Irregular galaxies can have chaotic-looking structures. Interactions and mergers between galaxies can strongly influence their shapes.

Galaxy Classification

Astronomers classify galaxies partly by their visible shapes and structures. Edwin Hubble developed an important early galaxy classification system.

Examples

🌠 Milky Way

Our home galaxy is a barred spiral galaxy containing hundreds of billions of stars.

🌌 Andromeda Galaxy

Andromeda is a large spiral galaxy and one of the nearest major galaxies to the Milky Way.

✨ Large Magellanic Cloud

The Large Magellanic Cloud is a nearby irregular galaxy that can be observed from Earth's southern skies.

Did you know?
Galaxies are not isolated islands in space. They can interact, collide, and merge with one another over enormous timescales. These interactions can dramatically change their shapes.
