Science.

Explore the systematic study of nature and the universe, from particles and forces to living organisms, planets, stars, galaxies and the fundamental laws that connect them.

What Is Science?

Science is a systematic way of developing knowledge about the natural world through observation, measurement, experimentation, mathematical reasoning and evidence.

Observation

Scientists carefully observe natural phenomena and collect information about them.

Evidence

Scientific explanations are supported by measurements, experiments and reproducible evidence.

Reasoning

Scientists use logic and mathematics to develop, test and refine explanations.

The Scientific Method

The scientific method provides a structured approach for investigating questions about the natural world.

01

Ask a Question

Begin with a question about a phenomenon that can be investigated.

02

Develop a Hypothesis

Propose a testable explanation or prediction based on existing knowledge.

03

Experiment

Design observations or experiments that can test the hypothesis.

04

Analyze

Examine the collected data using appropriate mathematical and statistical methods.

05

Communicate

Scientific findings are shared so other researchers can evaluate, reproduce and build upon them.

Scientific Research Pipeline

A simplified representation of how scientific investigation can progress from a question to new knowledge.

QUESTION ↓ HYPOTHESIS ↓ EXPERIMENT ↓ DATA ↓ ANALYSIS ↓ CONCLUSION ↓ NEW QUESTIONS

Major Branches of Science

Science contains many interconnected disciplines, each focusing on different aspects of nature.

Physics

Studies matter, energy, motion, forces, fields, space, time and the fundamental laws of nature.

Chemistry

Studies matter, its composition, properties, structure and chemical transformations.

Biology

Studies living organisms, their structures, functions, evolution and interactions.

Astronomy

Studies stars, planets, galaxies, black holes, cosmic structures and the evolution of the universe.

Earth Science

Studies Earth's atmosphere, oceans, rocks, climate, geological processes and natural systems.

Mathematics

Provides powerful structures and methods for describing patterns, quantities and relationships.

Physics

Physics seeks to understand the fundamental behavior of matter, energy, space and time.

Mechanics

Studies motion, forces, momentum, energy and the behavior of physical objects.

Electromagnetism

Describes electric charges, electric fields, magnetic fields and electromagnetic waves.

Thermodynamics

Studies heat, temperature, energy transfer and the behavior of macroscopic systems.

Quantum Physics

Studies physical systems at atomic and subatomic scales.

Relativity

Describes space, time, motion, gravity and their relationship at high speeds and strong gravity.

Particle Physics

Investigates the fundamental particles and interactions that make up the physical universe.

Chemistry

Chemistry explores how atoms and molecules interact to form the enormous variety of materials found in nature.

Atoms

Atoms are the basic units of chemical elements, containing nuclei and electrons.

Molecules

Molecules consist of atoms connected through chemical bonds.

Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions rearrange atoms and form new substances.

Organic Chemistry

Focuses largely on carbon-containing compounds and their reactions.

Materials Chemistry

Studies chemical structures and properties of materials used in technology and industry.

Biochemistry

Explores the chemical processes occurring within living organisms.

Biology

Biology investigates living systems, from molecules and cells to organisms, ecosystems and evolution.

Cell Biology

Studies cells, their structures, functions and interactions.

Genetics

Studies heredity, genes and the transmission of biological information.

Evolution

Explains how populations change over generations through processes including natural selection.

Neuroscience

Studies the nervous system, brain, neurons and biological mechanisms of behavior and cognition.

Ecology

Studies relationships between organisms and their environments.

Microbiology

Studies microorganisms such as bacteria, archaea, fungi and microscopic eukaryotes.

Astronomy & Space Science

Astronomy investigates objects and phenomena beyond Earth, while space science also examines the environment around our planet and throughout the Solar System.

Stars

Stars are enormous astronomical objects powered primarily by nuclear fusion in their interiors.

Planets

Planets are large celestial bodies that orbit stars and can have widely different physical environments.

Galaxies

Galaxies contain stars, gas, dust, dark matter and other structures bound together by gravity.

Black Holes

Regions of spacetime can become so strongly gravitationally bound that light cannot escape from within their event horizons.

Cosmology

Studies the origin, structure, evolution and large-scale behavior of the universe.

Exoplanets

Exoplanets are planets outside our Solar System that orbit other stars.

Earth Science

Earth science studies the planet's physical systems and the processes that shape its surface and interior.

Geology

Studies Earth's rocks, minerals, structures and geological history.

Meteorology

Studies Earth's atmosphere and the processes that produce weather.

Oceanography

Studies Earth's oceans, including their physical, chemical and biological systems.

Climate Science

Studies long-term patterns and changes in Earth's climate system.

Seismology

Studies earthquakes and the propagation of seismic waves through Earth.

Environmental Science

Examines interactions between natural systems and human activities.

Mathematics & Science

Mathematics provides languages, structures and tools that scientists use to describe and analyze natural phenomena.

Algebra

Provides methods for representing relationships between quantities and variables.

Geometry

Studies shapes, spaces, dimensions and mathematical relationships.

Calculus

Provides tools for studying change, motion, accumulation and continuous systems.

Statistics

Helps scientists analyze uncertainty, variation and patterns in data.

Probability

Provides mathematical tools for reasoning about uncertainty and random events.

Computational Science

Uses computers, algorithms and simulations to investigate scientific problems.

Science & Technology

Scientific discoveries provide knowledge about nature, while engineering and technology use that knowledge to create practical systems and tools.

Physics → Electronics

Understanding electricity and semiconductors enables modern electronic systems.

Biology → Biotechnology

Biological knowledge can be combined with engineering to develop new technologies.

Computer Science → AI
