Mechanics.

Explore motion, forces, energy, momentum, rotation, gravitation and the fundamental laws that describe how objects move and interact.

What Is Mechanics?

Mechanics is a major branch of physics concerned with the motion of objects and the forces and interactions that influence that motion. It forms the foundation for much of classical physics and is essential for engineering, astronomy and many areas of science.

Core Areas of Mechanics

🏃

Kinematics

Describes motion using quantities such as position, displacement, velocity and acceleration without focusing on the causes of motion.

💪

Dynamics

Studies how forces and interactions affect the motion of objects.

⚖️

Statics

Deals with objects and systems in equilibrium, where the relevant forces and torques balance.

🔄

Rotational Mechanics

Studies the rotation of bodies, including angular displacement, angular velocity, torque and moment of inertia.

🌍

Gravitation

Describes gravitational attraction between masses and explains phenomena such as planetary orbits.

🚀

Orbital Mechanics

Applies mechanics and gravitation to the motion of spacecraft, satellites, planets and other orbiting objects.

Describing Motion

Motion can be described using several fundamental physical quantities.

Position

The location of an object relative to a chosen reference point or coordinate system.

Displacement

The change in position of an object. It is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction.

Velocity

The rate at which displacement changes with time.

v = Δx / Δt

Acceleration

The rate at which velocity changes with time.

a = Δv / Δt

Newton's Laws of Motion

1

Newton's First Law — Inertia

An object remains at rest or continues in uniform motion in a straight line when the net external force acting on it is zero.

2

Newton's Second Law

The net force on an object is related to the rate of change of its momentum. For constant mass:

F = ma
3

Newton's Third Law

Forces between interacting bodies occur in pairs. The forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction, acting on different objects.

Work, Energy & Power

⚙️ Work

In a simple constant-force case, work is related to the component of force along the displacement.

W = Fd cosθ

🏃 Kinetic Energy

The energy associated with the motion of an object.

K = ½mv²

🏔️ Potential Energy

Energy associated with an object's position or configuration in a force field.

⚡ Power

Power measures the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.

P = W / t

Momentum & Collisions

Momentum is a fundamental vector quantity associated with the motion of an object.

Momentum

For an object of mass m moving with velocity v:

p = mv

Impulse

Impulse is equal to the change in momentum.

J = Δp

Conservation of Momentum

For an isolated system, total momentum remains constant when the net external impulse is zero.

Circular Motion

🔄 Angular Velocity

Describes how quickly angular position changes with time.

🎯 Centripetal Acceleration

An object moving in a circular path has an inward acceleration toward the center.

aᶜ = v² / r

💫 Centripetal Force

The required net inward force for circular motion.

Fᶜ = mv² / r

Gravitation

Every mass attracts every other mass through gravity. Newton's law of universal gravitation describes this attraction in the classical approximation.

🌍 Newton's Law of Gravitation

The gravitational force between two point masses is proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation.

F = Gm₁m₂ / r²

Mechanics in the Real World

🚗 Vehicles

Mechanics helps engineers understand acceleration, braking, friction, suspension and vehicle stability.

🏗️ Engineering

Structures and machines are designed using concepts involving forces, equilibrium, torque and motion.

🚀 Spaceflight

Orbital mechanics helps determine trajectories, orbital speeds and spacecraft motion under gravity.

⚽ Sports

Projectile motion, momentum, rotation and friction can all be used to analyze sporting movements.

⚙️ QENOAIX Fact

Mechanics connects everyday observations with mathematical laws. The same fundamental principles can describe a falling object on Earth, the motion of a satellite and the trajectory of a spacecraft.
